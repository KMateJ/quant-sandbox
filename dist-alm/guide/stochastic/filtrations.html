Guides/Stochastic models/04

Filtrations, adapted processes and stopping times

Lesson 4 of 17 · 1 min read

The lesson "Probability spaces, σ-algebras and random variables" modelled a state of information as a σ-algebra and showed that, on a finite sample space, a σ-algebra is a partition into atoms. Prices are observed over time, so information grows. This lesson makes that growth precise with filtrations, defines which processes are known at each time (adapted) or one step ahead (predictable), and defines stopping times, the random times at which one can act without seeing the future. Three tosses of a fair coin serve as the running example.

Filtrations

Fix a probability space (Ω,F,P)(\Omega, \mathcal{F}, \mathbb{P})(Ω,F,P) and an index set of times III, either {0,1,2,…}\{0, 1, 2, \ldots\}{0,1,2,…} or [0,∞)[0, \infty)[0,∞). A filtration is a family (Ft)t∈I(\mathcal{F}_t)_{t \in I}(Ft​)t∈I​ of σ-algebras with

Fs⊆Ft⊆Ffor all s≤t.\mathcal{F}_s \subseteq \mathcal{F}_t \subseteq \mathcal{F} \qquad \text{for all } s \le t.Fs​⊆Ft​⊆Ffor all s≤t.

Ft\mathcal{F}_tFt​ is the collection of events whose occurrence is decided by time ttt. The inclusion says that information is never lost: an event that is decided at time sss is still decided at any later time. The quadruple (Ω,F,(Ft),P)(\Omega, \mathcal{F}, (\mathcal{F}_t), \mathbb{P})(Ω,F,(Ft​),P) is a filtered probability space.

Given a process (Xt)t∈I(X_t)_{t \in I}(Xt​)t∈I​, its natural filtration is the information generated by its own history,

FtX=σ(Xs:s≤t),\mathcal{F}^X_t = \sigma(X_s : s \le t),FtX​=σ(Xs​:s≤t),

the smallest σ-algebra for which every XsX_sXs​ with s≤ts \le ts≤t is measurable. A filtration may be larger than the natural one, for example when an investor also watches other assets, but never smaller if the investor sees XXX.

Adapted processes

A process (Xt)(X_t)(Xt​) is adapted to (Ft)(\mathcal{F}_t)(Ft​) if XtX_tXt​ is Ft\mathcal{F}_tFt​-measurable for every ttt: its current value is known from the current information. Predictable processes

A discrete-time process (Hk)k≥1(H_k)_{k \ge 1}(Hk​)k≥1​ is predictable if HkH_kHk​ is Fk−1\mathcal{F}_{k-1}Fk−1​-measurable for every k≥1k \ge 1k≥1: it is known one step before the time it is indexed by. Stopping times

A stopping time is a random time τ:Ω→I∪{∞}\tau : \Omega \to I \cup \{\infty\}τ:Ω→I∪{∞} such that

{τ≤t}∈Ftfor every t∈I.\{\tau \le t\} \in \mathcal{F}_t \qquad \text{for every } t \in I.{τ≤t}∈Ft​for every t∈I.

Whether τ\tauτ has already happened by time ttt must be decided by the information at time ttt. The value τ=∞\tau = \inftyτ=∞ means the time never occurs. In discrete time the condition is equivalent to {τ=k}∈Fk\{\tau = k\} \in \mathcal{F}_k{τ=k}∈Fk​ for every kkk. Proof: if all {τ≤k}∈Fk\{\tau \le k\} \in \mathcal{F}_k{τ≤k}∈Fk​, then {τ=k}={τ≤k}∖{τ≤k−1}\{\tau = k\} = \{\tau \le k\} \setminus \{\tau \le k-1\}{τ=k}={τ≤k}∖{τ≤k−1} is in Fk\mathcal{F}_kFk​ because Fk−1⊆Fk\mathcal{F}_{k-1} \subseteq \mathcal{F}_kFk−1​⊆Fk​. Conversely, {τ≤k}=⋃j≤k{τ=j}\{\tau \le k\} = \bigcup_{j \le k} \{\tau = j\}{τ≤k}=⋃j≤k​{τ=j}, a union of events from Fj⊆Fk\mathcal{F}_j \subseteq \mathcal{F}_kFj​⊆Fk​.

A stopping time is a rule for when to act that uses only the past and present: {τ≤t}∈Ft\{\tau \le t\} \in \mathcal{F}_t{τ≤t}∈Ft​ for all ttt. Exercise dates of American options and stop-loss triggers are stopping times; "sell at the highest price of the year" is not.

Simple facts follow directly from the definition. A constant τ=c\tau = cτ=c is a stopping time, since {τ≤t}\{\tau \le t\}{τ≤t} is ∅\emptyset∅ or Ω\OmegaΩ. If τ\tauτ and ρ\rhoρ are stopping times, so are min⁡(τ,ρ)\min(\tau, \rho)min(τ,ρ) and max⁡(τ,ρ)\max(\tau, \rho)max(τ,ρ), because {min⁡(τ,ρ)≤t}={τ≤t}∪{ρ≤t}\{\min(\tau,\rho) \le t\} = \{\tau \le t\} \cup \{\rho \le t\}{min(τ,ρ)≤t}={τ≤t}∪{ρ≤t} and {max⁡(τ,ρ)≤t}={τ≤t}∩{ρ≤t}\{\max(\tau,\rho) \le t\} = \{\tau \le t\} \cap \{\rho \le t\}{max(τ,ρ)≤t}={τ≤t}∩{ρ≤t}.

First hitting times

Let XXX be adapted and AAA a Borel set. The first hitting time of AAA in discrete time is τA=min⁡{k≥0:Xk∈A}\tau_A = \min\{k \ge 0 : X_k \in A\}τA​=min{k≥0:Xk​∈A}, with τA=∞\tau_A = \inftyτA​=∞ if XXX never enters AAA. It is a stopping time because

{τA≤k}=⋃j=0k{Xj∈A}∈Fk,\{\tau_A \le k\} = \bigcup_{j=0}^{k} \{X_j \in A\} \in \mathcal{F}_k,{τA​≤k}=j=0⋃k​{Xj​∈A}∈Fk​,

Its law is P(τ=k)=2−k\mathbb{P}(\tau = k) = 2^{-k}P(τ=k)=2−k for k=1,2,3k = 1, 2, 3k=1,2,3 and P(τ=∞)=18\mathbb{P}(\tau = \infty) = \tfrac18P(τ=∞)=81​, so P(τ≤2)=12+14=0.75\mathbb{P}(\tau \le 2) = \tfrac12 + \tfrac14 = 0.75P(τ≤2)=21​+41​=0.75.

Last exit times

Now let σ\sigmaσ be the time of the last head among the three tosses, with no value if all three are tails. It is not a stopping time. At k=1k = 1k=1, {σ≤1}={HTT}\{\sigma \le 1\} = \{HTT\}{σ≤1}={HTT}, which is not a union of atoms of F1\mathcal{F}_1F1​: the outcomes HTT and HTH agree on the first toss, yet σ(HTT)=1≤1\sigma(HTT) = 1 \le 1σ(HTT)=1≤1 and σ(HTH)=3>1\sigma(HTH) = 3 > 1σ(HTH)=3>1. At k=2k = 2k=2, {σ≤2}={HHT,HTT,THT}\{\sigma \le 2\} = \{HHT, HTT, THT\}{σ≤2}={HHT,HTT,THT} splits the atoms HH, HT and TH of F2\mathcal{F}_2F2​. To know at time 1 that the last head has already happened, you would need to see tosses 2 and 3.

The σ-algebra at a stopping time

The information available at a stopping time τ\tauτ is the σ-algebra

Fτ={A∈F:A∩{τ≤t}∈Ft for all t},\mathcal{F}_\tau = \{A \in \mathcal{F} : A \cap \{\tau \le t\} \in \mathcal{F}_t \text{ for all } t\},Fτ​={A∈F:A∩{τ≤t}∈Ft​ for all t},

the events whose occurrence is known once τ\tauτ has happened. For τ\tauτ = first head, the event "toss 2 was H" belongs to Fτ\mathcal{F}_\tauFτ​ only together with the knowledge of τ\tauτ: on {τ=1}\{\tau = 1\}{τ=1} it is not yet decided, on {τ=2}\{\tau = 2\}{τ=2} it is. The strong Markov property and optional stopping, in later lessons, are statements about Fτ\mathcal{F}_\tauFτ​.

Worked example

Conditioning on the first of two coin tosses

  1. Two fair tosses, XXX = number of heads, F1=σ(ξ1)\mathcal{F}_1 = \sigma(\xi_1)F1​=σ(ξ1​). If the first toss is H, one head is in and the second adds 12\tfrac1221​ on average.

    E[X∣F1]=1+12on {ξ1=H}\displaystyle \mathbb{E}[X \mid \mathcal{F}_1] = 1 + \tfrac12 \quad \text{on } \{\xi_1 = H\}E[X∣F1​]=1+21​on {ξ1​=H}=1.5

Summary

  • A filtration (Ft)(\mathcal{F}_t)(Ft​) is an increasing family of σ-algebras; Ft\mathcal{F}_tFt​ is the information at time ttt, and the natural filtration σ(Xs:s≤t)\sigma(X_s : s \le t)σ(Xs​:s≤t) is the history of XXX.
  • With nnn coin tosses, Fk\mathcal{F}_kFk​ is the partition into 2k2^k2k atoms of outcomes that agree on the first kkk tosses, with 22k2^{2^k}22k events.
  • Adapted means XtX_tXt​ is Ft\mathcal{F}_tFt​-measurable; predictable means HkH_kHk​ is Fk−1\mathcal{F}_{k-1}Fk−1​-measurable, the form of a trading position chosen in advance.
  • A stopping time satisfies {τ≤t}∈Ft\{\tau \le t\} \in \mathcal{F}_t{τ≤t}∈Ft​. First hitting times are stopping times; last exit times are not.
  • For Brownian motion, FtW\mathcal{F}^W_tFtW​ is independent of future increments, and first passage times are stopping times.
