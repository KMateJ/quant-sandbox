The reflection principle and first passage times

Lesson 7 of 17 · 1 min read

This lesson computes the distribution of the running maximum of Brownian motion and of the first time it reaches a given level. The tool is the reflection principle, which rests on the strong Markov property from "Properties of Brownian motion" and on the notion of a stopping time from "Filtrations, adapted processes and stopping times". The results are exact closed forms, and they are the basis of barrier option pricing.

Running maximum and first passage time

Let WWW be a standard Brownian motion with natural filtration (FtW)(\mathcal{F}^W_t)(FtW​) and fix a level a>0a > 0a>0. Define the running maximum and the first passage time

Mt=max⁡0≤s≤tWs,τa=inf⁡{t≥0:Wt=a}.M_t = \max_{0 \le s \le t} W_s, \qquad \tau_a = \inf\{t \ge 0 : W_t = a\}.Mt​=0≤s≤tmax​Ws​,τa​=inf{t≥0:Wt​=a}.

The maximum exists because the path is continuous on the compact interval [0,t][0, t][0,t]. Since W0=0<aW_0 = 0 < aW0​=0<a and paths are continuous, the intermediate value theorem gives the identity of events

{τa≤t}={Mt≥a},Wτa=a  on {τa<∞}.\{\tau_a \le t\} = \{M_t \ge a\}, \qquad W_{\tau_a} = a \ \text{ on } \{\tau_a < \infty\}.{τa​≤t}={Mt​≥a},Wτa​​=a  on {τa​<∞}.

τa\tau_aτa​ is a stopping time. By continuity, Mt≥aM_t \ge aMt​≥a holds exactly when for every nnn some rational q∈[0,t]q \in [0, t]q∈[0,t] has Wq>a−1/nW_q > a - 1/nWq​>a−1/n, so

{τa≤t}=⋂n≥1 ⋃q∈Q∩[0,t]{Wq>a−1/n}∈FtW,\{\tau_a \le t\} = \bigcap_{n \ge 1}\ \bigcup_{q \in \mathbb{Q} \cap [0,t]} \{W_q > a - 1/n\} \in \mathcal{F}^W_t,{τa​≤t}=n≥1⋂​ q∈Q∩[0,t]⋃​{Wq​>a−1/n}∈FtW​,

a countable combination of events that are each known at time ttt. By recurrence, τa<∞\tau_a < \inftyτa​<∞ almost surely; this also follows from the formula below.

The reflection argument

Define the reflected path: follow WWW until it first reaches aaa, then mirror it in the line y=ay = ay=a:

W~t={Wt,t≤τa,2a−Wt,t>τa.\widetilde W_t = \begin{cases} W_t, & t \le \tau_a, \\ 2a - W_t, & t > \tau_a. \end{cases}Wt​={Wt​,2a−Wt​,​t≤τa​,t>τa​.​

Claim: W~\widetilde WW is again a standard Brownian motion. This is where the strong Markov property enters. Applied at the stopping time τa\tau_aτa​, it says that Bs=Wτa+s−aB_s = W_{\tau_a + s} - aBs​=Wτa​+s​−a, s≥0s \ge 0s≥0, is a Brownian motion independent of Fτa\mathcal{F}_{\tau_a}Fτa​​, and in particular of the stopped path (Wt∧τa)t≥0(W_{t \wedge \tau_a})_{t \ge 0}(Wt∧τa​​)t≥0​. By symmetry −B-B−B is also a Brownian motion independent of Fτa\mathcal{F}_{\tau_a}Fτa​​. Hence the pairs (stopped path, BBB) and (stopped path, −B-B−B) have the same joint law. WWW is obtained from the first pair by gluing a+Ba + Ba+B onto the stopped path at τa\tau_aτa​, and W~\widetilde WW from the second pair by the same gluing map, so WWW and W~\widetilde WW have the same law.

Both requirements are essential. At a fixed time sss the ordinary Markov property would suffice, but τa\tau_aτa​ is random. And the argument would fail for a random time that looks ahead, such as the time at which the maximum over [0,1][0, 1][0,1] is attained: after that time the path is forced to stay below its maximum, so it is not a fresh Brownian motion.

Now count paths. W~\widetilde WW reaches aaa at the same time τa\tau_aτa​ as WWW, and on {τa≤t}\{\tau_a \le t\}{τa​≤t} we have W~t=2a−Wt\widetilde W_t = 2a - W_tWt​=2a−Wt​, so Wt<aW_t < aWt​<a exactly when W~t>a\widetilde W_t > aWt​>a. Using that W~\widetilde WW has the law of WWW, and that {Wt>a}⊂{τa≤t}\{W_t > a\} \subset \{\tau_a \le t\}{Wt​>a}⊂{τa​≤t},

P(Mt≥a, Wt<a)=P(τa≤t, W~t>a)=P(τa≤t, Wt>a)=P(Wt>a).\mathbb{P}(M_t \ge a,\ W_t < a) = \mathbb{P}(\tau_a \le t,\ \widetilde W_t > a) = \mathbb{P}(\tau_a \le t,\ W_t > a) = \mathbb{P}(W_t > a).P(Mt​≥a, Wt​<a)=P(τa​≤t, Wt​>a)=P(τa​≤t, Wt​>a)=P(Wt​>a).

Since P(Wt=a)=0\mathbb{P}(W_t = a) = 0P(Wt​=a)=0, splitting {Mt≥a}\{M_t \ge a\}{Mt​≥a} by the sign of Wt−aW_t - aWt​−a gives the theorem.

Reflection principle: for a>0a > 0a>0 and t>0t > 0t>0, P(Mt≥a)=2 P(Wt≥a)=2(1−Φ(a/t))\mathbb{P}(M_t \ge a) = 2\,\mathbb{P}(W_t \ge a) = 2\big(1 - \Phi(a/\sqrt{t})\big)P(Mt​≥a)=2P(Wt​≥a)=2(1−Φ(a/t​)). In particular MtM_tMt​ has the same law as ∣Wt∣|W_t|∣Wt​∣.

The last statement holds because P(∣Wt∣≥a)=2 P(Wt≥a)\mathbb{P}(|W_t| \ge a) = 2\,\mathbb{P}(W_t \ge a)P(∣Wt​∣≥a)=2P(Wt​≥a) for a>0a > 0a>0. A fair picture is a mirror placed at height aaa: every path that touches aaa and ends below it has a mirror twin that ends above it, equally likely, so touching paths split evenly between the two sides. For the coin-flip walk the same argument with steps of ±1\pm 1±1 is exact and is André's classical solution of the ballot problem.

The law of the first passage time

Because {τa≤t}={Mt≥a}\{\tau_a \le t\} = \{M_t \ge a\}{τa​≤t}={Mt​≥a}, the reflection principle gives the distribution function of τa\tau_aτa​, and differentiating in ttt (using ddt at−1/2=−12at−3/2\tfrac{d}{dt}\,a t^{-1/2} = -\tfrac12 a t^{-3/2}dtd​at−1/2=−21​at−3/2) gives its density:

P(τa≤t)=2(1−Φ(at)),fτa(t)=a2πt3 e−a2/(2t),t>0.\mathbb{P}(\tau_a \le t) = 2\Big(1 - \Phi\Big(\frac{a}{\sqrt{t}}\Big)\Big), \qquad f_{\tau_a}(t) = \frac{a}{\sqrt{2\pi t^3}}\,e^{-a^2/(2t)}, \quad t > 0.P(τa​≤t)=2(1−Φ(t​a​)),fτa​​(t)=2πt3​a​e−a2/(2t),t>0.

This is the Lévy distribution. Two facts follow:

  • P(τa<∞)=lim⁡t→∞2(1−Φ(a/t))=2(1−Φ(0))=1\mathbb{P}(\tau_a < \infty) = \lim_{t \to \infty} 2(1 - \Phi(a/\sqrt{t})) = 2(1 - \Phi(0)) = 1P(τa​<∞)=limt→∞​2(1−Φ(a/t​))=2(1−Φ(0))=1: every level is reached almost surely.
  • E[τa]=∞\mathbb{E}[\tau_a] = \inftyE[τa​]=∞: for large ttt, t fτa(t)≈a (2π)−1/2 t−1/2t\,f_{\tau_a}(t) \approx a\,(2\pi)^{-1/2}\,t^{-1/2}tfτa​​(t)≈a(2π)−1/2t−1/2, which is not integrable on [1,∞)[1, \infty)[1,∞).

The level is certain to be reached, but the expected waiting time is infinite. The median is finite: P(τa≤t)=12\mathbb{P}(\tau_a \le t) = \tfrac12P(τa​≤t)=21​ when a/t=Φ−1(0.75)≈0.6745a/\sqrt{t} = \Phi^{-1}(0.75) \approx 0.6745a/t​=Φ−1(0.75)≈0.6745, that is t≈2.198 a2t \approx 2.198\,a^2t≈2.198a2. The factor a2a^2a2 is scaling at work: τa\tau_aτa​ has the same law as a2τ1a^2\tau_1a2τ1​. This also explains a point from "Conditional expectation and martingales": WWW is a martingale with W0=0W_0 = 0W0​=0, yet Wτa=aW_{\tau_a} = aWτa​​=a. Stopping at τa\tau_aτa​ turns a fair game into a sure gain only because τa\tau_aτa​ is unbounded with infinite mean, so the optional stopping theorem does not apply.

Joint law of the maximum and the endpoint

The same reflection gives more. For a≥0a \ge 0a≥0 and x≤ax \le ax≤a, on {τa≤t}\{\tau_a \le t\}{τa​≤t} the condition Wt≤xW_t \le xWt​≤x is equivalent to W~t≥2a−x\widetilde W_t \ge 2a - xWt​≥2a−x, and 2a−x≥a2a - x \ge a2a−x≥a forces τa≤t\tau_a \le tτa​≤t. Hence

P(Mt≥a, Wt≤x)=P(Wt≥2a−x)=1−Φ(2a−xt),a≥0, x≤a.\mathbb{P}(M_t \ge a,\ W_t \le x) = \mathbb{P}(W_t \ge 2a - x) = 1 - \Phi\Big(\frac{2a - x}{\sqrt{t}}\Big), \qquad a \ge 0,\ x \le a.P(Mt​≥a, Wt​≤x)=P(Wt​≥2a−x)=1−Φ(t​2a−x​),a≥0, x≤a.

Differentiating in aaa and xxx gives the joint density

fMt,Wt(m,w)=2(2m−w)2πt3 exp⁡(−(2m−w)22t),m≥0, w≤m.f_{M_t, W_t}(m, w) = \frac{2(2m - w)}{\sqrt{2\pi t^3}}\,\exp\Big(-\frac{(2m - w)^2}{2t}\Big), \qquad m \ge 0,\ w \le m.fMt​,Wt​​(m,w)=2πt3​2(2m−w)​exp(−2t(2m−w)2​),m≥0, w≤m.

For example, the probability that the path reaches 1 by time 1 but ends at or below 0 is P(W1≥2)=1−Φ(2)≈0.0228\mathbb{P}(W_1 \ge 2) = 1 - \Phi(2) \approx 0.0228P(W1​≥2)=1−Φ(2)≈0.0228.

This joint law is what barrier options need. An up-and-out call pays (ST−K)+(S_T - K)^+(ST​−K)+ only if the price stays below a barrier HHH throughout [0,T][0, T][0,T]. Under the Black–Scholes model the log price is a drifted Brownian motion, and the price of the option is a discounted integral of the payoff against the joint density of the maximum and the endpoint, adjusted for the drift as above. The closed-form barrier prices used in practice come from exactly this computation.

Summary

  • τa=inf⁡{t:Wt=a}\tau_a = \inf\{t : W_t = a\}τa​=inf{t:Wt​=a} is a stopping time and {τa≤t}={Mt≥a}\{\tau_a \le t\} = \{M_t \ge a\}{τa​≤t}={Mt​≥a}.
  • The strong Markov property at τa\tau_aτa​ makes the path reflected after τa\tau_aτa​ a Brownian motion; this gives P(Mt≥a)=2(1−Φ(a/t))\mathbb{P}(M_t \ge a) = 2(1 - \Phi(a/\sqrt{t}))P(Mt​≥a)=2(1−Φ(a/t​)) and Mt∼∣Wt∣M_t \sim |W_t|Mt​∼∣Wt​∣.
  • τa\tau_aτa​ has the Lévy density a(2πt3)−1/2e−a2/(2t)a(2\pi t^3)^{-1/2}e^{-a^2/(2t)}a(2πt3)−1/2e−a2/(2t): it is finite almost surely but has infinite mean.
  • P(Mt≥a,Wt≤x)=1−Φ((2a−x)/t)\mathbb{P}(M_t \ge a, W_t \le x) = 1 - \Phi((2a - x)/\sqrt{t})P(Mt​≥a,Wt​≤x)=1−Φ((2a−x)/t​) for x≤ax \le ax≤a; this joint law prices barrier options.
