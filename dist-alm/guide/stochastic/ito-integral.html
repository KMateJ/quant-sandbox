The Itô integral

Lesson 9 of 17 · 1 min read

This lesson defines the stochastic integral ∫0tHs dWs\int_0^t H_s\,dW_s∫0t​Hs​dWs​, which gives meaning to the expression dX=a dt+b dWdX = a\,dt + b\,dWdX=adt+bdW used for asset prices. It builds on "Filtrations, adapted processes and stopping times" (adapted integrands), "Conditional expectation and martingales" (the martingale property) and "Properties of Brownian motion" (infinite total variation and quadratic variation [W]t=t[W]_t = t[W]t​=t). The next lesson, "Itô's lemma", is the chain rule for these integrals.

Why a Riemann–Stieltjes integral fails

For a deterministic function ggg, the Riemann–Stieltjes integral ∫0tf dg\int_0^t f\,dg∫0t​fdg is the limit of sums ∑if(si) (g(ti+1)−g(ti))\sum_i f(s_i)\,(g(t_{i+1}) - g(t_i))∑i​f(si​)(g(ti+1​)−g(ti​)) over partitions 0=t0<t1<⋯<tn=t0 = t_0 < t_1 < \dots < t_n = t0=t0​<t1​<⋯<tn​=t whose mesh max⁡i(ti+1−ti)\max_i (t_{i+1} - t_i)maxi​(ti+1​−ti​) tends to zero, with any evaluation points si∈[ti,ti+1]s_i \in [t_i, t_{i+1}]si​∈[ti​,ti+1​]. A classical theorem says that this limit exists for every continuous fff exactly when ggg has finite total variation. Brownian paths have infinite total variation on every interval, almost surely, so the pathwise construction is not available.

The failure is visible in the simplest case f=g=Wf = g = Wf=g=W. Write ΔWi=Wti+1−Wti\Delta W_i = W_{t_{i+1}} - W_{t_i}ΔWi​=Wti+1​​−Wti​​ and compare right-point and left-point sums:

∑iWti+1 ΔWi−∑iWti ΔWi=∑i(ΔWi)2⟶t.\sum_{i} W_{t_{i+1}}\,\Delta W_i - \sum_{i} W_{t_i}\,\Delta W_i = \sum_{i} (\Delta W_i)^2 \longrightarrow t.i∑​Wti+1​​ΔWi​−i∑​Wti​​ΔWi​=i∑​(ΔWi​)2⟶t.

The difference tends to the quadratic variation ttt, not to zero, so the answer depends on where in each interval the integrand is evaluated. A definition has to fix the evaluation point. Itô's choice is the left endpoint.

The Itô isometry

Theorem. For a simple HHH,

E ⁣[(∫0THs dWs)2]=E ⁣[∫0THs2 ds].\mathbb{E}\!\left[\Big(\int_0^T H_s\,dW_s\Big)^{2}\right] = \mathbb{E}\!\left[\int_0^T H_s^2\,ds\right].E[(∫0T​Hs​dWs​)2]=E[∫0T​Hs2​ds].

Proof. Let I=∑iξi ΔWiI = \sum_i \xi_i\,\Delta W_iI=∑i​ξi​ΔWi​ and expand the square into diagonal and cross terms. On the diagonal, ξi2\xi_i^2ξi2​ is Fti\mathcal{F}_{t_i}Fti​​-measurable and ΔWi\Delta W_iΔWi​ is independent of Fti\mathcal{F}_{t_i}Fti​​ with variance ti+1−tit_{i+1} - t_iti+1​−ti​, so by the tower property

E[ξi2(ΔWi)2]=E[ξi2 E[(ΔWi)2∣Fti]]=E[ξi2] (ti+1−ti).\mathbb{E}\big[\xi_i^2 (\Delta W_i)^2\big] = \mathbb{E}\Big[\xi_i^2\,\mathbb{E}\big[(\Delta W_i)^2 \mid \mathcal{F}_{t_i}\big]\Big] = \mathbb{E}[\xi_i^2]\,(t_{i+1} - t_i).E[ξi2​(ΔWi​)2]=E[ξi2​E[(ΔWi​)2∣Fti​​]]=E[ξi2​](ti+1​−ti​).

Extension to general integrands

Let LT2\mathcal{L}^2_TLT2​ be the set of progressively measurable processes (adapted and jointly measurable in (s,ω)(s, \omega)(s,ω)) with E[∫0THs2 ds]<∞\mathbb{E}\big[\int_0^T H_s^2\,ds\big] < \inftyE[∫0T​Hs2​ds]<∞. The two integrals have complementary strengths. Stratonovich integrals obey the ordinary chain rule, which is why they are common in physics, where white noise models a smooth but fast physical signal. Itô integrals are martingales: E[∫0tW dW]=0\mathbb{E}\big[\int_0^t W\,dW\big] = 0E[∫0t​WdW]=0, while E[∫0tW∘dW]=t/2\mathbb{E}\big[\int_0^t W \circ dW\big] = t/2E[∫0t​W∘dW]=t/2. Finance uses Itô for a modelling reason. A trader chooses the position ξi\xi_iξi​ at the start of the period, using the information Fti\mathcal{F}_{t_i}Fti​​, and then the price moves. The gain ξi (Sti+1−Sti)\xi_i\,(S_{t_{i+1}} - S_{t_i})ξi​(Sti+1​​−Sti​​) is a left-point sum. Evaluating at the midpoint would let the position depend on half of the price move it is paid for, which is a form of trading on future information. Under a measure where discounted prices are martingales, the Itô integral keeps the gains from any such strategy a martingale too, which is the continuous-time statement that no strategy turns a fair game into a sure profit.

Summary

  • Brownian paths have infinite variation, so ∫H dW\int H\,dW∫HdW cannot be defined path by path; the value of the sums depends on the evaluation point.
  • For simple adapted HHH the Itô integral is ∑iξi ΔWi\sum_i \xi_i\,\Delta W_i∑i​ξi​ΔWi​ with ξi\xi_iξi​ known at tit_iti​; the Itô isometry E[(∫H dW)2]=E[∫H2 ds]\mathbb{E}[(\int H\,dW)^2] = \mathbb{E}[\int H^2\,ds]E[(∫HdW)2]=E[∫H2ds] extends it to all of LT2\mathcal{L}^2_TLT2​ by L2L^2L2 limits.
  • The Itô integral is linear, has mean zero, is a martingale and has quadratic variation ∫H2 ds\int H^2\,ds∫H2ds.
