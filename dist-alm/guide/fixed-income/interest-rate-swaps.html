Interest rate swaps

Lesson 7 of 10 · 1 min read

An interest rate swap is the most traded interest rate derivative. Two parties exchange interest payments on the same amount: one pays a fixed rate, the other a floating rate that is reset every period. This lesson shows that a swap is a pair of bonds in disguise, prices it with the discount factors of "Spot rates, forward rates and the yield curve", and derives the par swap rate. It also uses bond pricing from "Bond prices and yields".

A plain-vanilla swap

A plain-vanilla interest rate swap is fixed by four things:

  • a notional NNN, the amount on which interest is calculated. It is never exchanged;
  • a fixed rate sss, agreed today for the whole life of the swap;
  • a floating rate LiL_iLi​ for each period, a short-term reference rate observed at the start of the period (the reset date) and paid at its end;
  • payment dates t1<t2<⋯<tnt_1 < t_2 < \dots < t_nt1​<t2​<⋯<tn​, with accrual fractions τi=ti−ti−1\tau_i = t_i - t_{i-1}τi​=ti​−ti−1​ in years (t0=0t_0 = 0t0​=0).

On each date tit_iti​ the fixed leg pays Ns τiN s\,\tau_iNsτi​ and the floating leg pays NLi τiN L_i\,\tau_iNLi​τi​. In practice only the difference changes hands. The party that pays fixed and receives floating holds a payer swap; the other side holds a receiver swap. Throughout this lesson payments are annual, so τi=1\tau_i = 1τi​=1, but the formulas keep τi\tau_iτi​ so they work for any frequency.

The swap as two bonds

Add a fictitious exchange of the notional NNN at maturity tnt_ntn​ on both legs. The two exchanges cancel, so the swap is unchanged. Now each leg is a bond:

  • the fixed leg is a fixed-rate bond with face value NNN and coupon s τis\,\tau_isτi​ per period;
  • the floating leg is a floating-rate note (FRN), a bond whose coupon is the floating rate of each period.

A receiver swap is therefore long the fixed-rate bond and short the FRN, and a payer swap is the reverse. Its value is the difference of two bond prices:

Vreceiver=Bfix−Bfloat,Vpayer=Bfloat−Bfix.V_{\text{receiver}} = B_{\text{fix}} - B_{\text{float}}, \qquad V_{\text{payer}} = B_{\text{float}} - B_{\text{fix}}.Vreceiver​=Bfix​−Bfloat​,Vpayer​=Bfloat​−Bfix​.

The floating-rate note on a reset date

Assume the floating rate LiL_iLi​ is the market rate for lending from ti−1t_{i-1}ti−1​ to tit_iti​, the same rate the discount factors are built from. Then an FRN is worth exactly its face value on every reset date. Work backwards from the end:

  1. At the last reset date tn−1t_{n-1}tn−1​ the note will pay N(1+Lnτn)N(1 + L_n\tau_n)N(1+Ln​τn​) at tnt_ntn​. Discounting one period at the same rate LnL_nLn​ gives N(1+Lnτn)/(1+Lnτn)=NN(1 + L_n\tau_n)/(1 + L_n\tau_n) = NN(1+Ln​τn​)/(1+Ln​τn​)=N.
  2. At tn−2t_{n-2}tn−2​ the note pays the coupon NLn−1τn−1N L_{n-1}\tau_{n-1}NLn−1​τn−1​ at tn−1t_{n-1}tn−1​ and is then worth NNN. Together that is N(1+Ln−1τn−1)N(1 + L_{n-1}\tau_{n-1})N(1+Ln−1​τn−1​), which again discounts to NNN.
  3. Repeating the argument back to today gives Bfloat=NB_{\text{float}} = NBfloat​=N at t0=0t_0 = 0t0​=0.

The intuition: the coupon always pays the current market rate, so the note never trades away from par at a reset. Between reset dates its value moves only a little, because the next coupon is already fixed.

The fixed leg and the par swap rate

Write P(0,t)P(0,t)P(0,t) for the discount factor, the price today of 1 paid at ttt. The fixed-rate bond is a set of known cash flows, so

Bfix=Ns∑i=1nτi P(0,ti)+N P(0,tn).B_{\text{fix}} = N s \sum_{i=1}^{n} \tau_i\,P(0,t_i) + N\,P(0,t_n).Bfix​=Nsi=1∑n​τi​P(0,ti​)+NP(0,tn​).

The sum A=∑iτiP(0,ti)A = \sum_i \tau_i P(0,t_i)A=∑i​τi​P(0,ti​) is the annuity factor: the value today of receiving 1 per year on the payment dates. A new swap is agreed at the fixed rate that makes it worth zero to both sides, Bfix=Bfloat=NB_{\text{fix}} = B_{\text{float}} = NBfix​=Bfloat​=N. Dividing by NNN and solving for that rate gives the par swap rate SnS_nSn​ of an nnn-period swap:

Sn=1−P(0,tn)∑i=1nτi P(0,ti)=1−P(0,tn)A.S_n = \frac{1 - P(0,t_n)}{\sum_{i=1}^{n} \tau_i\,P(0,t_i)} = \frac{1 - P(0,t_n)}{A}.Sn​=∑i=1n​τi​P(0,ti​)1−P(0,tn​)​=A1−P(0,tn​)​.

The par swap rate is the coupon of a bond priced at par: Sn=(1−P(0,tn))/AS_n = (1 - P(0,t_n))/ASn​=(1−P(0,tn​))/A, where A=∑iτiP(0,ti)A = \sum_i \tau_i P(0,t_i)A=∑i​τi​P(0,ti​) is the annuity factor.

The value of an existing swap

After a swap is agreed, rates move and its value is no longer zero. Suppose right after the trade above the whole curve shifts up by half a percentage point, and the discount factors become 0.95430.95430.9543, 0.90730.90730.9073 and 0.86060.86060.8606. The new annuity factor is A′=2.7222A' = 2.7222A′=2.7222, and the new par rate is S3′=0.1394/2.7222≈5.12%S_3' = 0.1394/2.7222 \approx 5.12\%S3′​=0.1394/2.7222≈5.12%.

Consider the payer swap with notional N=10,000,000N = 10{,}000{,}000N=10,000,000 that pays the contracted rate s=S3≈4.62%s = S_3 \approx 4.62\%s=S3​≈4.62%. The floating leg is still worth NNN; the fixed leg is now worth less. Its value is

Vpayer=N[(1−P(0,tn))−s A′]=N (Sn′−s) A′≈10,000,000×0.00499×2.7222≈135,900.V_{\text{payer}} = N\big[(1 - P(0,t_n)) - s\,A'\big] = N\,(S_n' - s)\,A' \approx 10{,}000{,}000 \times 0.00499 \times 2.7222 \approx 135{,}900.Vpayer​=N[(1−P(0,tn​))−sA′]=N(Sn′​−s)A′≈10,000,000×0.00499×2.7222≈135,900.

The payer gains because it locked in paying 4.62% when the market rate for the same swap is now 5.12%. The second form is worth remembering: an existing swap is worth the difference between the current and the contracted swap rate, paid on the annuity.

The same formula gives the swap's sensitivity. A change of one basis point (0.01%) in the swap rate changes the value by about NA×0.0001N A \times 0.0001NA×0.0001. This is the swap's DV01, here 10,000,000×2.7222×0.0001≈2,72210{,}000{,}000 \times 2.7222 \times 0.0001 \approx 2{,}72210,000,000×2.7222×0.0001≈2,722. It plays the role that duration played in "Duration and convexity".

Uses of swaps

  • Changing the type of debt. A firm that issued a fixed-rate bond and enters a receiver swap receives fixed and pays floating, so in total it pays a floating rate. A payer swap turns floating-rate debt into fixed.
  • Hedging interest rate risk. A bank whose assets are long fixed-rate loans can pay fixed on a swap so that a rise in rates no longer hurts it.
  • Expressing a view on rates. A payer swap gains when rates rise and a receiver swap gains when they fall, without buying or selling any bonds.

Summary

  • A swap exchanges fixed payments NsτiN s\tau_iNsτi​ for floating payments NLiτiN L_i\tau_iNLi​τi​ on a notional that is never exchanged.
  • A receiver swap is a long fixed-rate bond and a short floating-rate note; the note is worth par on every reset date.
  • The par swap rate is Sn=(1−P(0,tn))/AS_n = (1 - P(0,t_n))/ASn​=(1−P(0,tn​))/A with the annuity factor A=∑iτiP(0,ti)A = \sum_i \tau_i P(0,t_i)A=∑i​τi​P(0,ti​).
