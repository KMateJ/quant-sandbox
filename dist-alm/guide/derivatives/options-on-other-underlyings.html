Options on dividend-paying stocks, indices, currencies and futures

Lesson 13 of 17 · 1 min read

"The Black–Scholes formula" prices an option on a stock that pays no dividends. Real underlyings are more varied: stocks and indices pay dividends, a currency earns interest abroad, and a futures contract costs nothing to hold. This lesson shows that all of them need the same small change, and that the change is easiest to see through the forward price from "Arbitrage and the forward price".

A continuous dividend yield

Suppose the stock pays a dividend at a continuous rate qqq of its price. Holding one share for time TTT then also earns the dividends, which reinvested in the stock turn one share into eqTe^{qT}eqT shares at expiry. To end with exactly one share, replicate with only e−qTe^{-qT}e−qT shares today. The stock price that matters in the model is therefore the price net of dividends to come, S0e−qTS_0 e^{-qT}S0​e−qT, and the formula is the old one with that substitution:

c=S0e−qTN(d1)−Ke−rTN(d2),p=Ke−rTN(−d2)−S0e−qTN(−d1),c = S_0 e^{-qT} N(d_1) - K e^{-rT} N(d_2), \qquad p = K e^{-rT} N(-d_2) - S_0 e^{-qT} N(-d_1),c=S0​e−qTN(d1​)−Ke−rTN(d2​),p=Ke−rTN(−d2​)−S0​e−qTN(−d1​),
d1=ln⁡(S0/K)+(r−q+12σ2) TσT,d2=d1−σT.d_1 = \frac{\ln(S_0/K) + (r - q + \tfrac12\sigma^2)\,T}{\sigma\sqrt T}, \qquad d_2 = d_1 - \sigma\sqrt T.d1​=σT​ln(S0​/K)+(r−q+21​σ2)T​,d2​=d1​−σT​.

The forward price as the common thread

The forward price of the stock for delivery at TTT is F=S0e(r−q)TF = S_0 e^{(r-q)T}F=S0​e(r−q)T, as in "Arbitrage and the forward price" with the dividend income subtracted. Substitute S0e−qT=Fe−rTS_0 e^{-qT} = F e^{-rT}S0​e−qT=Fe−rT into the call formula and the yield disappears from view:

c=e−rT [ F N(d1)−K N(d2) ],d1=ln⁡(F/K)+12σ2TσT.c = e^{-rT}\,\bigl[\,F\,N(d_1) - K\,N(d_2)\,\bigr], \qquad d_1 = \frac{\ln(F/K) + \tfrac12\sigma^2 T}{\sigma\sqrt T}.c=e−rT[FN(d1​)−KN(d2​)],d1​=σT​ln(F/K)+21​σ2T​.

This is the one formula behind every case below. The option depends on the underlying only through its forward price FFF, the strike and the volatility; what changes from one underlying to the next is how FFF is built from today's price.

Index options

A stock index pays the dividends of its many members, which add up to a fairly smooth stream. Treating it as a continuous yield qqq is the standard approximation, and the formula above applies with S0S_0S0​ the index level. A single stock paying a few large dividends is different: a common fix replaces S0S_0S0​ by the price less the present value of the dividends due before expiry, and then uses the ordinary formula (the escrowed-dividend method). We do not derive it here.

Currency options: Garman–Kohlhagen

Prices are quoted in the domestic currency per unit of foreign currency, with domestic rate rdr_drd​ and foreign rate rfr_frf​. A holder of the foreign currency earns the foreign interest rate, which plays exactly the role of a dividend yield: q=rfq = r_fq=rf​. The forward exchange rate is F=S0e(rd−rf)TF = S_0 e^{(r_d - r_f)T}F=S0​e(rd​−rf​)T, the covered interest parity relation, and the call is e−rdT[FN(d1)−KN(d2)]e^{-r_d T}[F N(d_1) - K N(d_2)]e−rd​T[FN(d1​)−KN(d2​)]. This is the Garman–Kohlhagen formula.

Options on futures: Black-76

A futures contract needs no investment to enter, so holding it earns nothing and costs nothing to finance. Its price behaves like a stock paying a yield equal to the interest rate, q=rq = rq=r, so Fe−rTF e^{-rT}Fe−rT stands in for S0e−qTS_0 e^{-qT}S0​e−qT in the formula and the rate drops out of d1d_1d1​. With FFF the futures price, the call is again e−rT[FN(d1)−KN(d2)]e^{-rT}[F N(d_1) - K N(d_2)]e−rT[FN(d1​)−KN(d2​)]; this is the Black-76 formula, widely used for options on commodities, interest rate futures and, with forward yields, caps and swaptions. For the futures themselves see "Bond futures and the cheapest-to-deliver".

The figure uses a stock with strike 100 and spot 100, volatility 20% and one year to expiry. At the defaults (rate 5%, dividend yield 3%) the call costs 8.65 against 10.45 without a yield, the put costs 6.73, and the forward is 102.02. Raise the yield and watch the call curve sink towards the payoff line; set it to zero and the two curves coincide.

Put–call parity with a yield

The relation from "Put–call parity" survives with the same substitution: a call minus a put is a forward, valued at today's money,

c−p=S0e−qT−Ke−rT.c - p = S_0 e^{-qT} - K e^{-rT}.c−p=S0​e−qT−Ke−rT.

A call and a put on the same underlying, strike and expiry therefore carry the same information about the yield.

Summary

  • With a continuous yield qqq, replace S0S_0S0​ by S0e−qTS_0 e^{-qT}S0​e−qT in the Black–Scholes formula; d1d_1d1​ then has drift r−qr - qr−q.
  • Every case is one formula in the forward price: c=e−rT[FN(d1)−KN(d2)]c = e^{-rT}[F N(d_1) - K N(d_2)]c=e−rT[FN(d1​)−KN(d2​)] with F=S0e(r−q)TF = S_0 e^{(r-q)T}F=S0​e(r−q)T.
  • Indices use the dividend yield, currencies the foreign rate (Garman–Kohlhagen), futures q=rq = rq=r (Black-76).
  • Put–call parity becomes c−p=S0e−qT−Ke−rTc - p = S_0 e^{-qT} - K e^{-rT}c−p=S0​e−qT−Ke−rT.
