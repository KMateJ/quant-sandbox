Put–call parity

Lesson 4 of 17 · 1 min read

This lesson derives an exact relation between the prices of a European call and a European put. It combines the synthetic forward from "Combining positions" with the law of one price from "Arbitrage and the forward price". Like the forward price, the result needs no model for how the stock moves.

Setting

Take a European call and a European put on the same non-dividend-paying stock, with the same strike KKK and the same maturity TTT. Their prices today are C0C_0C0​ and P0P_0P0​. The stock trades at S0S_0S0​, and the continuously compounded risk-free rate is rrr, so a zero-coupon bond paying KKK at TTT costs Ke−rTKe^{-rT}Ke−rT today. The market assumptions are those of "Arbitrage and the forward price": no transaction costs, borrowing and lending at rrr, short selling allowed.

Two portfolios with the same payoff

From "Combining positions", a long call and a short put with the same strike pay

(ST−K)+−(K−ST)+=ST−K(S_T - K)^+ - (K - S_T)^+ = S_T - K(ST​−K)+−(K−ST​)+=ST​−K

for every value of STS_TST​. Now build two portfolios:

  • Portfolio A: buy one call and sell one put. Cost today C0−P0C_0 - P_0C0​−P0​. Payoff at TTT: ST−KS_T - KST​−K.
  • Portfolio B: buy one share and borrow Ke−rTKe^{-rT}Ke−rT, so that KKK is owed at TTT. Cost today S0−Ke−rTS_0 - Ke^{-rT}S0​−Ke−rT. Payoff at TTT: ST−KS_T - KST​−K.

Portfolio B is the stock-and-loan replication of a forward with delivery price KKK, as in the cash-and-carry argument. The two portfolios pay exactly the same amount in every state at TTT, so the law of one price says they cost the same today:

C0−P0=S0−K e−rT.C_0 - P_0 = S_0 - K\,e^{-rT}.C0​−P0​=S0​−Ke−rT.

This is put–call parity. Rearranged as C0+Ke−rT=P0+S0C_0 + Ke^{-rT} = P_0 + S_0C0​+Ke−rT=P0​+S0​, it reads: a call plus cash equal to the discounted strike is worth the same as a put plus the stock. Both sides pay max⁡(ST,K)\max(S_T, K)max(ST​,K) at maturity.

What parity does and does not tell you

Parity is model-free: it holds whatever the distribution of STS_TST​, because the argument never uses probabilities. It links the two prices, so knowing one gives the other. A desk that prices calls also prices puts, and the put price follows from the call price by P0=C0−S0+Ke−rTP_0 = C_0 - S_0 + Ke^{-rT}P0​=C0​−S0​+Ke−rT.

Parity does not tell you the level of either price. It fixes the difference C0−P0C_0 - P_0C0​−P0​, while the volatility of the stock determines how large both are. Finding that level needs a model of the stock price, which starts in "The one-step binomial model".

The result relies on its assumptions. With dividends, the stock leg must be reduced by the present value of the dividends. For American options, early exercise breaks the exact equality and leaves only inequalities.

Simple no-arbitrage bounds

The same reasoning bounds the call price without a model. For the upper bound, a call never pays more than the stock, since (ST−K)+≤ST(S_T - K)^+ \le S_T(ST​−K)+≤ST​ for K≥0K \ge 0K≥0. A portfolio that always pays less cannot cost more, so C0≤S0C_0 \le S_0C0​≤S0​.

For the lower bound, the call payoff is never negative, so C0≥0C_0 \ge 0C0​≥0. Also, (ST−K)+≥ST−K(S_T - K)^+ \ge S_T - K(ST​−K)+≥ST​−K, so the call is worth at least the forward-like portfolio B, which costs S0−Ke−rTS_0 - Ke^{-rT}S0​−Ke−rT. Combining:

max⁡(S0−K e−rT, 0)≤C0≤S0.\max\left(S_0 - K\,e^{-rT},\,0\right) \le C_0 \le S_0.max(S0​−Ke−rT,0)≤C0​≤S0​.

The lower bound also follows from parity: since P0≥0P_0 \ge 0P0​≥0, C0=P0+S0−Ke−rT≥S0−Ke−rTC_0 = P_0 + S_0 - Ke^{-rT} \ge S_0 - Ke^{-rT}C0​=P0​+S0​−Ke−rT≥S0​−Ke−rT. In the example the call must lie between 4.88 and 100. These bounds are wide; they exclude absurd prices but leave the actual level to a model.

Summary

  • A long call and a short put with the same strike and maturity pay ST−KS_T - KST​−K, the same as a share financed by borrowing Ke−rTKe^{-rT}Ke−rT.
  • By the law of one price, C0−P0=S0−Ke−rTC_0 - P_0 = S_0 - Ke^{-rT}C0​−P0​=S0​−Ke−rT.
  • Parity is model-free: it links the call and put prices but does not fix their level.
  • Without a model the call price is only bounded: max⁡(S0−Ke−rT,0)≤C0≤S0\max(S_0 - Ke^{-rT}, 0) \le C_0 \le S_0max(S0​−Ke−rT,0)≤C0​≤S0​.
