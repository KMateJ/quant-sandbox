Arbitrage and the forward price

Lesson 3 of 17 · 1 min read

This lesson finds the delivery price of a forward contract without any model for the future stock price. The argument rests on one assumption, that the market offers no arbitrage, and on the discount factor from "Interest, compounding and discounting". The forward itself was defined in "Forwards, calls and puts".

Market assumptions

We work in an idealised market. Anyone can borrow and lend at the same risk-free rate rrr (continuously compounded). The stock can be bought or sold short in any quantity; short selling means borrowing a share, selling it today and buying it back later to return it. There are no transaction costs, taxes or dividends, and both parties to a contract honour it.

Arbitrage

An arbitrage is a trading strategy that costs nothing to set up, can never lose money, and makes a positive profit in at least some outcomes. An equivalent form: a strategy that brings in money today and never requires a payment later.

Think of two shops selling the same item at different prices: buy in the cheap one, sell in the expensive one, and the profit is certain. In a liquid market, traders exploit such gaps as soon as they appear, and their buying and selling moves the prices until the gap closes. Pricing theory therefore assumes that arbitrage opportunities do not exist.

The law of one price

The most useful consequence is the law of one price: if two portfolios have the same payoff in every possible state at time TTT, they must have the same price today. Otherwise one would buy the cheaper portfolio and sell the dearer one. The payoffs cancel at TTT, and the price difference is kept today as a riskless profit.

No arbitrage implies the law of one price: two portfolios with identical payoffs at TTT have the same price today. Pricing by replication applies this rule again and again.

The cash-and-carry argument

Summary

  • An arbitrage costs nothing, never loses and sometimes gains; pricing theory assumes none exist.
  • No arbitrage implies the law of one price: equal payoffs at TTT mean equal prices today.
  • Replicating the forward with stock and a loan gives F0=S0erTF_0 = S_0e^{rT}F0​=S0​erT, independent of any view on STS_TST​.
  • A quote above F0F_0F0​ is exploited by cash-and-carry, a quote below by reverse cash-and-carry; the locked-in profit does not depend on STS_TST​.
  • An existing forward with delivery price KKK is worth ft=St−Ke−r(T−t)f_t = S_t - Ke^{-r(T-t)}ft​=St​−Ke−r(T−t).
