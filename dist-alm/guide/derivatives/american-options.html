American options and early exercise

Lesson 8 of 17 · 1 min read

All options priced so far could be exercised only at maturity. Many traded options, including most single-stock options, can be exercised at any time up to maturity. This lesson prices that extra right with the tree from "Multi-step trees and backward induction", shows why it is worthless for a call on a stock without dividends but valuable for a put, and explains how "Put–call parity" changes.

European and American exercise

A European option gives the right to exercise only at maturity TTT. An American option gives the right to exercise at any time t≤Tt \le Tt≤T. Exercising a call at time ttt pays St−KS_t - KSt​−K; exercising a put pays K−StK - S_tK−St​. This amount is the intrinsic value of the option at time ttt: (St−K)+(S_t - K)^+(St​−K)+ for a call and (K−St)+(K - S_t)^+(K−St​)+ for a put.

Throughout the lesson the stock pays no dividends, and rrr is the continuously compounded risk-free rate.

Two inequalities follow directly from the definition. An American option gives every right the European one gives, and more, so it is worth at least as much. And an American option can always be exercised immediately, so it is worth at least its intrinsic value. If it traded below that, buying it and exercising at once would be an arbitrage.

VtAm ≥ VtEu,VtAm ≥ intrinsic value at t.V^{\text{Am}}_t \ \ge\ V^{\text{Eu}}_t, \qquad V^{\text{Am}}_t \ \ge\ \text{intrinsic value at } t.VtAm​ ≥ VtEu​,VtAm​ ≥ intrinsic value at t.

Backward induction with early exercise

In a tree the holder of an American option decides at each node whether to exercise. Exercising gives the intrinsic value. Not exercising keeps the option alive one more step, which is worth the discounted risk-neutral average of the two successor values, the continuation value. A rational holder takes the larger of the two. Backward induction therefore changes in a single place:

Vi,j=max⁡((K−Si,j)+⏟exercise, e−rΔt[q Vi+1,j+1+(1−q) Vi+1,j]⏟continuation),V_{i,j} = \max\Big( \underbrace{(K - S_{i,j})^+}_{\text{exercise}},\ \underbrace{e^{-r\Delta t}\big[q\,V_{i+1,j+1} + (1-q)\,V_{i+1,j}\big]}_{\text{continuation}} \Big),Vi,j​=max(exercise(K−Si,j​)+​​, continuatione−rΔt[qVi+1,j+1​+(1−q)Vi+1,j​]​​),

written here for a put, with q=(erΔt−d)/(u−d)q = (e^{r\Delta t} - d)/(u - d)q=(erΔt−d)/(u−d) as before. At maturity the value is the payoff. Nodes where the exercise value is larger form the exercise region: if the stock reaches such a node, the holder exercises there.

No early exercise of a call

For a call on a stock without dividends the extra right is worthless. A European call is bounded below by the no-arbitrage bound from "Put–call parity":

CtEu ≥ St−Ke−r(T−t) > St−K(r>0, t<T).C^{\text{Eu}}_t \ \ge\ S_t - K e^{-r(T-t)} \ >\ S_t - K \qquad (r > 0,\ t < T).CtEu​ ≥ St​−Ke−r(T−t) > St​−K(r>0, t<T).

So even a European call, which cannot be exercised before TTT, is worth more than the St−KS_t - KSt​−K that early exercise would give. Selling the call is always better than exercising it, and the American call has the same price as the European one. The reason is that exercising early pays KKK sooner than necessary, which loses interest, and gives up the protection the option provides if the stock falls.

For a put the interest argument works the other way. Exercising early receives KKK sooner, and the cash then earns interest. When the stock is far below the strike, the interest on KKK outweighs the remaining chance of a further fall, and early exercise is optimal. The early-exercise premium PAm−PEuP^{\text{Am}} - P^{\text{Eu}}PAm−PEu is therefore positive for a put.

The exercise boundary

For an American put, the exercise region at each time ttt consists of all stock prices below a critical level S∗(t)S^*(t)S∗(t), the exercise boundary. Above S∗(t)S^*(t)S∗(t) the holder waits; at or below it, the holder exercises. The boundary lies below KKK and rises towards KKK as ttt approaches TTT, because with little time left there is little reason to wait. It is not given by a closed-form formula; the tree finds it numerically as the dividing line between exercise and continuation nodes.

Summary

  • An American option can be exercised at any time; it is worth at least the European option and at least its intrinsic value.
  • In a tree, each node takes the larger of the intrinsic value and the discounted risk-neutral continuation value.
  • An American call on a stock without dividends is never exercised early, so it has the European price; an American put can be worth more than the European put.
  • Early exercise of a put is optimal below an exercise boundary S∗(t)<KS^*(t) < KS∗(t)<K that rises towards KKK near maturity.
  • Put–call parity becomes the bounds S0−K≤CAm−PAm≤S0−Ke−rTS_0 - K \le C^{\text{Am}} - P^{\text{Am}} \le S_0 - Ke^{-rT}S0​−K≤CAm−PAm≤S0​−Ke−rT.
