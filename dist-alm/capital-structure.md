# Modigliani–Miller Capital Structure Visualizer

Modigliani–Miller with and without taxes: levered cost of equity, WACC and the interest tax shield as leverage changes.

## About this tool

Modigliani and Miller showed that, without taxes and frictions, a firm's value does not depend on how it is financed. This tool shows how the cost of equity rises with leverage, how WACC stays flat without taxes, and how the interest tax shield lowers WACC and adds value once corporate taxes are introduced.

## Frequently asked questions

### Why does the cost of equity rise with leverage?

More debt makes the remaining equity riskier, because shareholders bear the business risk on a smaller base. Their required return rises exactly enough to keep WACC unchanged in the no-tax case.

### What is the interest tax shield?

The tax saving from deducting interest payments. With corporate taxes it makes debt financing cheaper and adds its present value to the firm.

### What are Modigliani–Miller propositions I and II?

Proposition I says that without taxes, bankruptcy costs or other frictions, a firm's value does not depend on its mix of debt and equity. Proposition II says the cost of equity rises linearly with leverage: r_e = r_A + (D/E)(r_A − r_d), where r_A is the return on the firm's assets. Together they mean cheaper debt is exactly offset by more expensive equity.

### Is there an optimal capital structure?

With corporate taxes alone, more debt always adds value through the tax shield. In practice the expected costs of financial distress and bankruptcy grow with leverage, so the trade-off theory places the optimum where the marginal tax benefit of debt equals its marginal expected distress cost.
