# Time Value of Money & Compound Interest Calculator

Compound growth of a principal and regular contributions over time, splitting the final balance into interest earned and money paid in.

## About this tool

The time value of money says a unit of money today is worth more than the same amount later, because it can earn interest. This compound interest calculator grows a starting principal and regular contributions over time and splits the final balance into the money you paid in and the interest it earned.

## Frequently asked questions

### Why does compound interest grow faster over time?

Interest is earned on previously earned interest as well as on the principal, so the balance grows exponentially rather than linearly.

### What is the difference between present and future value?

Future value compounds an amount forward at the interest rate; present value discounts a future amount back to today. They are the same calculation in opposite directions.

### How do you calculate compound interest?

Future value = P × (1 + r/n)^(n·t), where P is the principal, r the annual rate, n the number of compounding periods per year and t the number of years. For example, 1,000 at 5% compounded annually grows to 1,000 × 1.05¹⁰ ≈ 1,628.89 after 10 years.

### What is the rule of 72?

A quick estimate of the doubling time: divide 72 by the interest rate in percent. At 6% a year, money doubles in about 72 / 6 = 12 years; the exact answer is 11.9. The rule works well for rates between about 4% and 12%.
