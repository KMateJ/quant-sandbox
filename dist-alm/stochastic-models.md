# Stochastic Calculus Course: Random Walks to Brownian Motion

Interactive stochastic calculus lessons: random walks, the central limit theorem, the Wiener process, the reflection principle and first passage times, learned by experiment.

## About this tool

A hands-on course in stochastic processes and stochastic calculus. Each lesson poses a question and lets you answer it by experiment: from the symmetric random walk and the √t scaling law, through the central limit theorem and Donsker's theorem, to the Wiener process, its increments and covariance, the running maximum, the reflection principle and first passage times.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do I need to know stochastic calculus beforehand?

No. The lessons start from coin flips and build up step by step; each one has an Explore tab with experiments and a Mathematics tab with the precise statements.

### Why does Brownian motion scale with √t?

Its increments over disjoint intervals are independent, so their variances add. Variance therefore grows linearly in time and the typical deviation, the standard deviation, grows like √t.

### What is a Wiener process?

The mathematical name for standard Brownian motion: a continuous random path that starts at 0, has independent increments, and whose increment over a time span t is normally distributed with mean 0 and variance t. It arises as the limit of a rescaled random walk (Donsker's theorem) and is the source of randomness in most continuous-time financial models.

### What is the reflection principle?

For Brownian motion started at 0 and a level a > 0, every path that reaches a can be mirrored after its first hitting time, which gives P(max of W on [0, t] ≥ a) = 2 P(W_t ≥ a). It yields the distribution of the running maximum and of first passage times, which are used to price barrier options.
