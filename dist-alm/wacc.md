# WACC Calculator: Weighted Average Cost of Capital

Compute the weighted average cost of capital: cost of equity, after-tax cost of debt, the tax shield and how leverage changes WACC.

## About this tool

The weighted average cost of capital is the blended return a company must earn for its equity and debt holders. This WACC calculator combines the cost of equity and the after-tax cost of debt by their market-value weights, and shows how the interest tax shield and leverage move the result.

## Frequently asked questions

### Why is the cost of debt taken after tax?

Interest payments are tax-deductible, so the effective cost of debt to the firm is r_d × (1 − tax rate).

### What is WACC used for?

It is the discount rate for a company's free cash flows in a DCF valuation and the hurdle rate for projects with the same risk as the firm.

### How do you calculate WACC with taxes?

WACC = E/V × r_e + D/V × r_d × (1 − T), where E and D are the market values of equity and debt, V = E + D and T is the corporate tax rate. For example, 60% equity at 10%, 40% debt at 5% and a 25% tax rate give 0.6 × 10% + 0.4 × 5% × 0.75 = 7.5%.

### How is the cost of equity estimated?

Most often with CAPM: r_e = r_f + β × market risk premium. With a 3% risk-free rate, a beta of 1.2 and a 5% market risk premium, r_e = 3% + 1.2 × 5% = 9%. Unlike debt, equity has no stated interest rate, so its cost is inferred from the return investors require for the risk they bear.
